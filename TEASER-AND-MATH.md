TEMPORAL REFLECTION CHAMBER

Send in a pulse. Switch the medium. Test the returning wave.

Building on published temporal-reflection research, our proposed 8 × 8 × 8-foot enclosure would house a transmission line with synchronized electronic switches.

The goal: detect reversed pulse order, a predicted frequency change, and return timing that follows the switching event. The effect would occur in the engineered line inside the chamber.

One ideal example: quadruple the line’s capacitance while keeping its inductance fixed, and the converted frequency halves.

## Relevant math

For a spatially uniform temporal step in an ideal nondispersive transmission line, with fixed inductance per unit length:

\[
\frac{|f_2|}{|f_1|}=\sqrt{\frac{C'_1}{C'_2}},\qquad C'_2=4C'_1\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{|f_2|}{|f_1|}=\frac12.
\]

Here C′ is capacitance per unit length. An illustrative 40 MHz component would shift to 20 MHz; the reversed envelope’s feature spacings would double. These are ideal model predictions, not measured results. Actual amplitudes, bandwidth and timing depend on the implemented switching circuit, dispersion and losses.

Research precedent: Moussa et al., Nature Physics (2023), DOI 10.1038/s41567-023-01975-y. Public manuscript: https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07236 .
